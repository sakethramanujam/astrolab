Methods & reliability

How much should you trust a candidate?

We measure the pipeline instead of assuming it works: thousands of fake planets injected into real light curves (completeness), the same search run on flipped and scrambled light curves where no planet can exist (false alarms), and every vetting check scored against known planets and known false positives. The pipeline is frozen and versioned, so the numbers belong to exact code.

1 · The frozen pipeline

Detrend. Quality-flagged cadences dropped; biweight trend (0.75-day window) divided out; 0.3 days cut at each gap edge; upward outliers clipped; binned to 10 minutes.

Search. Box Least Squares over periods from 0.5 days to half the baseline and 8 durations (1–6 h). Each peak is scored with S = δ / (CDPPD / √Ntr): the box depth over the star's own noise on that duration, measured from the light curve, so stars with correlated (red) noise are not over-trusted. SDE is still logged but no longer used as the threshold.

Screens. At least 3 transits, odd/even depths agree, no secondary eclipse, not too deep or too long for the star, no brightening at half phase, not a period shared by many unrelated stars.

Deep checks. Difference-image centroid, Gaia DR3 neighbours, eclipsing-binary catalogues, V-shape, a secondary-eclipse scan, size limit, and new in v1: the star's density implied by the transit shape compared with the TIC catalogue, plus Gaia RUWE and non-single-star warnings.

3 · False alarms: what does pure noise look like?

Flip every light curve upside down (after removing flares) and a real planet becomes a brightening the search cannot fit; whatever the frozen search still finds is noise or instrument systematics. Scrambling day-long chunks destroys periodic signals too, but with one sector that test is limited. The histogram is the best peak of every star. 4 · How good is each vetting check?

For signals we can label (our peak matches a confirmed or known planet, or a known false positive or eclipsing binary) we count how often each check fires. A good check fires on false positives (amber bar) and almost never on planets (teal bar). Thresholds were tuned only on the tuning stars; the randomly held-out 20% of stars test them.

# v1.1 issue: odd/even and secondary tests reject deep real transits

Status: open. **v1.0.0 vetting is unchanged.** These tests stay as they are for every frozen v1.0.0 product.
This note records what we found and the proposed fix.

## Finding (injection–recovery, sector 70, 1,996 injections from run 0)

- `odd_even` rejects more matched injections as the expected SNR rises. The rate is 3–10% below SNR 100
  and 25% above SNR 100. `secondary`, `too_deep` and `variable` show the same pattern: rejection grows
  with depth.
- The test is **significance only**. `exosearch/vet.py` flags `odd_even` when
  `|d_odd - d_even| / hypot(e_odd, e_even) > 3`. There is no minimum fractional difference.
  `secondary` is also `d_sec / e_sec > 3` with no relative-depth condition.
- The errors are `astropy` `compute_stats` errors built from the per-cadence PDCSAP uncertainties of the
  binned light curve. They are white-noise errors, while detection now uses the red-noise-aware CDPP_D.
  When beta_D > 1 the odd/even significance is overstated, and the effect grows with SNR.
- We re-ran 30 matched injections with expected SNR > 50 (20 that failed `odd_even`, 10 that passed) and
  recorded `depth_odd` and `depth_even`. The failing cases have odd/even differences of 3.0–16σ but only
  **0.4–8.5% of the depth (median 2.6%)**. A 1–3% odd/even mismatch is far below any eclipsing-binary
  signature. It is produced by per-epoch sampling (10-min bins vs ingress/egress), local detrending of
  deep transits, and white-noise errors. None of the 30 had partial transits near gaps.
- **The injected ephemeris does not drift.** Injections use a fixed P and T0 (batman, e = 0, BTJD
  throughout), so nothing drifts inside the injection. The *recovered* ephemeris drifts by a median of
  0.06 transit durations over the sector (|P_rec − P_inj| × N_epochs / T14). The `odd_even` failure rate
  barely depends on that drift: 15% at < 0.1 durations and 23% at 0.1–0.25. Drift is not the cause.

## Proposed v1.1 change (agreed with the Scientist)

1. **Require both significance and size.** Flag `odd_even` only if the difference is > 3σ **and**
   `|d_odd − d_even| / mean(d_odd, d_even) > 10%`. Flag `secondary` only if it is > 3σ **and**
   `d_sec / d_primary > 10%`, or the secondary is significant at an absolute depth that cannot come from
   planetary reflection or thermal emission. A purely sigma-based test becomes oversensitive at high SNR.
   With the 10% condition, none of the 20 failing high-SNR injections above would fail.
2. Compute the σ in both tests from the scatter of per-epoch depths (or CDPP_D at the trial duration), not
   from per-cadence pipeline errors.
3. Re-measure the vetting effectiveness, i.e. the per-check true/false rejection rates on labelled
   planets and EBs (tuning stars only), before freezing v1.1. Plan to add an EB/blend injection set so
   that the loss in EB rejection is measured, not assumed.
4. `too_deep` (depth > 5%) and `variable` (phase-0.5 brightening > 5σ) cause the same high-SNR loss for
   giant planets and grazing geometries. Review them at the same time, with the same "significance and
   size" logic for `variable`.

## Also for v1.1: normalisation of S (white-noise calibration)

`python -m exosearch.whitenoise` ran 300 stars of pure Gaussian noise with the real S70 cadence and gaps
through the full chain. Results:

- **The overlapping-window MAD estimator for CDPP_D is unbiased.** Its ratio to the actual scatter of the
  D-window means is 0.996–1.006 for every D.
- The frozen 0.75-d biweight lowers that scatter below the white-noise truth by 2% at 1 h, rising to 17%
  at 6 h. That is real noise suppression, not an estimator bias.
- Per-trial S on white noise is over-dispersed. Its standard deviation is 1.01 at D = 1 h, rising to
  1.22 at 6 h.
  - Part comes from the box contrast keeping variance that the local running-mean CDPP_D no longer has
    after detrending.
  - The other part comes from the final S (coverage-weighted N_tr) leaving out the out-of-transit
    baseline error. At duty cycles D/P up to 0.5 this matters: S/S_scan is a median 1.16 for peaks
    with N_tr > 10.
- Max S on white noise has a median of 5.48 (S_scan 5.26, p95 5.95). The Gaussian extreme-value median
  for the analytic number of independent (P, T0, D) boxes (3.4 × 10^5) is 4.61. The per-D
  over-dispersion explains most of the difference.
- Fix: include the out-of-transit term in S. Then either normalise S per duration with the white-noise
  calibration (z-score per D), or measure CDPP_D from the scatter of the box contrast itself. Re-derive S*.

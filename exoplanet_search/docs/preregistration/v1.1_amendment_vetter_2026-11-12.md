## The four rules
Three are taken from the Kepler DR25 Robovetter (Thompson et al. 2018, ApJS 235, 38); (d) is new.
* **(a) Secondary-eclipse exemption.** A secondary does not reject when the geometric albedo it implies,
  A_g = depth_sec / (Rp/a)^2, is below 1 and Rp < 30 R_Earth. (Rp/a)^2 = primary depth / (a/R*)^2; a/R* from the TIC
  stellar density (circular orbit), else P / (pi x duration). depth_sec is the box depth at the secondary scan's own
  phase (deep check `secondary_scan`) or the phase-0.5 depth (screen `secondary`).
* **(b) Radius cap 30 R_Earth (about 2.7 R_Jup).** It replaces BOTH earlier caps: the deep check `rp_gt_2rjup`
  (2.0 R_Jup) and the screen `rp_too_large` (25 R_Earth).
* **(c) Stellar-density mismatch is a flag, not a rejection.**
* **(d) Leave-one-orbit-out secondary test (new).** A `secondary_scan` rejection stands only if the scan, re-run with
  any single orbit left out, still clears its 5 sigma threshold: min over the leave-one-orbit-out scans >= 5.
  Motivated by K2-34 b in sector 71: its 8.6 sigma secondary comes from one 5,150 ppm event in the first partial orbit
  (BTJD 3237.64); without that orbit the scan is 3.3 sigma; no phase-curve or ellipsoidal signal (P and P/2 harmonics
  90 +/- 25 and 56 +/- 24 ppm). Its false-alarm cost is paid by refitting S* on the nulls with the amended vetter.
Every other check and threshold is unchanged (odd-even, phase-0.5 secondary significance, V-shape, centroid, known EB,
momentum dumps, few transits, common period).
